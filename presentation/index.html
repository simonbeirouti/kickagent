The Challenge

How do you make hype visible on a KICK stream?

  • Every stream has energy — chat popping off, raids, gifted subs.
  • But that energy is invisible: it lives in the chat scroll for seconds, then it's gone.
  • The brief: give that energy a face, live, on the stream.
The Problem We Chose

A solo streamer is doing five jobs at once

  • Performing · reading chat · moderating · producing · engaging — live, simultaneously.
  • Big streamers have a mod team, a producer, a camera operator.
  • The streamer starting at zero viewers has nobody.
Our Hype Tracker

Meet the Kick-Assistant

Viewers see

  • Hype meter on stream — OBS-ready overlay: colour bands, session peak, live sparkline
  • Hit Me, Kick Me — live dares & bets, resolved by measured hype impact (WON / LOST / PUSH)
  • Auto highlights — clip markers captured at peak hype; the recap reel builds itself
  • Memes & alerts — fired at the peak moment, never a dead one

Only the streamer sees

  • Hype Score 0–100 — self-calibrating: "unusually busy for this channel, right now"
  • Pivot suggestions — "Pivot to 'buffet' — chat is already drifting there"
  • Trending gaps — "'slots' is trending on KICK, your chat hasn't touched it"
  • Spam shield — flags floods, never a fast-typing fan

One engine underneath all of it. Exciting without distracting: the busy stuff is streamer-only.

Under the Hood

Hype = "unusually busy for this channel, right now"

  • Self-calibrating z-score against a rolling baseline → identical at 200 viewers and 50,000. Test-proven: the same relative burst scores 91 at 1 msg/s and 82 at 40 msg/s.
  • Zero dependencies, O(1) per event. Signals: chat velocity, gifted kicks, subs, follows — weighted, per-user saturated.
  • Spam shield is duplicate-based, with flag expiry — volume alone never flags. A spam flood barely moves the score.
  • Honest by design: the scripted replay is deterministic stage insurance — and live mode runs the same engine on real KICK chat.
